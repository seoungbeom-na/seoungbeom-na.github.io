Digital Transformation Strategies: A Technology Roadmap in the Korean Water Industry

디지털 전환 전략: 국내 물산업 기술 로드맵
Seoungbeom Na, Chang-Geun Lee, Jae-Wan Park, Woosik Jang, Youngwoong Lee · DOI 10.3390/su18136745

Digital transformation of the water industry requires many technologies at once, and budgets do not allow all of them. Which management areas and technologies should be developed first?

01Key figures and tables

FIG 01

How the roadmap was built

Management areas are defined from the literature, evaluated by expert survey on four criteria, checked for reliability with Cronbach's alpha, then ranked by Euclidean distance and frequency analysis.

TBL 02

Sector priority and leading technologies

RankManagement areaLeading technologies
1Natural environment management and predictionWater management forecasting and operation, source water quality management, water information survey and analysis
2Water supply and customer serviceIntelligent network management, digital customer service, water supply
3Water intake and resource facilitiesEnergy generation, construction management of water resource facilities, intake source water quality management
4Water purificationWater treatment, wastewater treatment, water reuse

Natural environment management and prediction ranks first because it is the enabling layer for everything that follows. Energy generation leads within the water intake sector, where it scored highest on combined preference, technology level, and potential.

FIG 03

The resulting roadmap

Technologies are placed across four stages from short to long term, with markers for development preference, technology level, and potential. Short-term public investment goes to environmental prediction, while energy generation is positioned for the medium term alongside water and energy nexus policy.

02What it means

The contribution is a defensible sequence rather than a ranking: utilities can point to measured expert data when justifying which technology is funded first.
