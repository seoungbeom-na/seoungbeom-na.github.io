How Large Should Railway Solar Be? A Real Options Analysis of Scale Flexibility under SMP and REC Uncertainty

철도 태양광의 최적 규모: SMP·REC 불확실성 하 규모 유연성의 실물옵션 분석
Seoungbeom Na, Chang-Geun Lee, Kwangpil Park, Woosik Jang · DOI 10.3390/en19163890

Solar development on railway corridors faces two moving prices at once: the electricity market price (SMP) and the certificate price (REC). Under that double uncertainty, what capacity should actually be built?

Railway idle land deployment

The Honam Line combines soundproof walls, tunnels, median dividers, and embankment slopes into a 438 MW deployment. Existing structures carry the full REC weight of 1.0, while embankment slopes on general ground carry 0.8.

A three-month moving average smooths the 2022 energy crisis spike before log returns are taken, yielding an annual volatility of 18.42% for the combined SMP and REC selling price.

02What it means

The static DCF places the project only marginally above break-even, but the option to scale up is worth roughly fifty times the base project value. Because falling capital costs and rising panel efficiency are expected to clear the break-even margin with room to spare, railway idle land solar should be built with the capacity to grow rather than as a small pilot.
